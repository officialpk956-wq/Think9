## 2. ⚠️ The most important finding: "30+ brands" is aspirational

The challenge brief opens with *"we are building 30+ consumer brands simultaneously."*

Public evidence points to **11–14 ventures today**, not 30+.

Possible reconciliations: they may be counting the 75+ consulting brand journeys; counting sub-brands and product lines; or stating a forward target.

**How to handle this — important:**

Do **not** correct them in the submission. It reads as point-scoring and you may simply be missing internal context.

Instead, turn it into a design principle: **"built at 11 brands, designed for 30+."** Argue that the correct time to install a cross-portfolio intelligence layer is *before* the portfolio triples, because building the canonical graph at 11 brands is cheap and doing it at 30 is a migration project. This shows you noticed, respects their framing, and produces a genuinely stronger "why now" than the brief itself supplies.

**The honest read:** the portfolio is heterogeneous. Roughly **5–6 brands form a tight CPG/beauty cluster** with genuine packaging and component overlap. Media IP and retail formats do not source the same inputs.

This is a real constraint on a sourcing pitch and you should name it before they do. Scope the pitch explicitly to the CPG cluster — **SuperYou, Goodbug, Panchamrit, Neude, Beauty by Bie** — and name those brands. Naming real portfolio brands in the deck is a strong credibility signal and almost no other candidate will do it.

## 5. 🎯 The single biggest strategic discovery: "Office of Optimisation"

Think9 already runs a vertical called the **Office of Optimisation**, alongside Consulting, Venture Studio, and The Foundery.

Its published playbooks include:
- Classical Mathematical Optimization
- Constraint Optimization
- Discrete Decision Optimization
- **Complex Systems Optimization** — explicitly *"supply chain or retail management"*
- Strategic & Multi-Goal Optimization
- **Optimization in Data Science & AI**

Its published application areas explicitly include **Supply Chain Optimization**, Automation, Process Optimization, and Benchmarking.

**Two things follow.**

**(a) Your pitch has a natural organisational home.** Most candidates will propose a system with no owner. You can say precisely where LOOM sits, who runs it, and which existing mandate it extends. That is a large, cheap advantage.

**(b) The unit looks nascent.** Its case-study slots are still unfilled placeholder text on the live site — *"This is a placeholder which will be updated by a KPI for SuperYou's optimisation case study."* Read carefully, that is an org that has declared the ambition and not yet shipped the proof. Your 30-day plan ending in **one real, banked negotiation saving** is precisely the artifact that gap is asking for.

Do not say "your case studies are empty." Do say: "LOOM is designed to sit inside the Office of Optimisation and produce its first supply-chain case study within 30 days."

## 6. AI is already an explicit company principle

From the About page, listed among Think9's stated principles:

> **"AI will redefine agility for business — making paths to growth speedy and seamless."**

Worth quoting directly in slide 1. Note the framing is **agility and speed**, not cost reduction. That validates the choice of *quote-to-decision cycle time* as the north-star metric over pure savings.

Other stated principles worth knowing: *"The old comes back in new forms"*; *"Impermanence will be a feature not a bug"*; *"Legacy categories are the low-hanging fruit for redefined propositions"*; *"Clarity over complexity"*; *"Less is more."*

Those last two are a direct steer on **how to present**: they explicitly value simplicity. A dense 40-slide deck argues against their stated values. Keep it tight.

## 7. Recent moves

**Whoppl acquisition → Nova9 (announced 18 Mar 2026).** Think9 acquired creator-marketing firm Whoppl (founded 2019, Ramya Ramachandran) and launched **Nova9**, a culture-led storytelling unit.

Santosh Desai, on Nova9's hiring plans:
> *"Nova9 will expand its capabilities by hiring across multiple disciplines, including cultural strategists, creative storytellers, meme and community specialists, platform-native content strategists, and **AI data analysts focused on real-time cultural signals**."*

**Strategic implication — this matters for track choice.** Nova9 is already staffing AI against real-time cultural signals. That means **Track 1 (Consumer Intelligence) and Track 4 (Creative Engine) may overlap with work already in flight.** Pitching them risks proposing something they've begun. Track 2 sits in a different vertical (Office of Optimisation) with no announced AI programme — cleaner ground.

**Acquisition posture.** As of Jan 2023 Think9 was in talks with ~12 D2C brands across clothing, FMCG, cosmetics, Ayurveda, home décor, children's furniture, general retail. Combined with the Whoppl deal, growth is partly inorganic — which strengthens the "new brand inherits portfolio-best pricing on day one" argument considerably. An acquired brand arrives with its own vendor list and its own naming conventions; canonical resolution is exactly the tool that absorbs it.

## 9. Context to understand but NOT to raise

Think9 was founded by Kishore Biyani's daughters following the collapse of Future Group — India's most prominent retail empire failure. Trade press has framed Think9 in redemption terms (e.g. The Ken: *"Biyani's retail empire flamed out. His daughters seek redemption in birthing new brands"*).

**Do not reference this in your submission or interview.** It is not yours to bring up.

But understand what it implies: this leadership has lived through a large retail organisation losing control of operational and financial complexity at scale. An argument about **portfolio-level operational visibility before complexity compounds** will land with unusual force here — as long as it is made on its own merits and never framed as a lesson from their family history.

## 10. What this changes in the submission

**Confirmed — keep Track 2.** Reinforced by: an existing Office of Optimisation with supply chain in scope but no shipped case study; consumer-goods operating depth on the board; and Nova9 already occupying the consumer-signal/creative ground that Tracks 1 and 4 would compete with.

**Six concrete changes to make:**

1. **Reframe as "built at 11, designed for 30+."** Turns the portfolio-size gap from a weakness into the "why now."
2. **Scope explicitly to the CPG cluster** and name SuperYou, Goodbug, Panchamrit, Neude, Beauty by Bie. Address heterogeneity before they raise it.
3. **Place LOOM inside the Office of Optimisation.** Name the owner. Frame it as delivering that unit's first supply-chain case study.
4. **Open with their own AI principle** — *"AI will redefine agility"* — and tie the north-star metric to agility, not cost.
5. **Lead the compounding argument with acquisitions.** Given inorganic growth, "an acquired brand's vendor list is absorbed and repriced within days" is a sharper hook than the generic new-brand case.
6. **Cut length.** They state "Clarity over complexity" and "Less is more" as values. A tight deck is itself an argument that you understood them.
